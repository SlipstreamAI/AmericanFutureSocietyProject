# Research method

## Name the kind of claim

| Type | Meaning | Appropriate use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation | Something directly inspected or measured, with its source and date | Repository state, a published record, a quoted price with its limits |
| Assumption | A chosen input used because the answer is not yet established | Scenario population, a test margin, adoption timing |
| Hypothesis | A proposition research can support or disprove | Hosting income may offset a portion of a rural parcel's costs |
| Proposal | A possible design or next action | A resident trust or a parcel-based pilot |
| Finding | An answer supported by a stated method and evidence, with uncertainty | A documented site constraint or a reproducible sensitivity result |
| Decision | A choice explicitly adopted by the operator, with scope and rationale | Which alternative to investigate next |

Confidence and authority are separate. A strongly supported finding can remain unapproved for implementation. An approved assumption can remain uncertain.

## Evidence expectations

For material inputs, record the original publisher, title, direct link, release or effective date, access date, period covered, geography, unit, method, limitations, and permitted use. Cite a page, table, section, or dataset identifier sufficiently specific to reproduce the claim. Prefer primary records; use secondary summaries to locate them or explain disagreement.

For location work, use the actual parcel, county, utility service territory, and water authority. A national map or state-level rule is a screening aid until the responsible authority and local conditions are verified. For commercial cases, distinguish vendor claims from operating records and independent performance evidence. For legal or health research, identify where qualified local or clinical review is required before relying on an implementation proposal.

## A complete research brief

State the question and decision it informs; the boundary and period; alternatives; method; evidence; calculations; assumptions; counterevidence; uncertainty; affected departments; and the next discriminating step. Include a result that could change the recommendation. Report unknowns as unknowns rather than assigning a favorable default.

## Quantitative discipline

- Specify nominal or real dollars and the price year. Do not mix annual, monthly, household, resident, worker, and recipient denominators.
- Separate stocks from flows, debt proceeds from revenue, capital expenditure from operating expense, and accounting profit from available cash.
- Reconcile transfers between entities and avoid counting the same payment as new value twice.
- Use distributions and cohort outcomes where averages hide people who lose. Show entry, exit, retirement, illness, housing tenure, and unequal ownership.
- Record range, sensitivity, dependencies, and source quality. Stress correlated failures rather than varying every input independently.
- An accounting check establishes consistency under assumptions. It does not establish demand, financing, legality, resource capacity, or predictive accuracy.

## Review and corrections

Self-check the evidence and arithmetic, then seek authorized specialist review when the task warrants it. Record material disagreement and its implications. Correct a superseded conclusion with a dated note and a link; do not erase the history of why an earlier decision was made. Update [findings](FINDINGS.md), [assumptions](ASSUMPTIONS.md), [decisions](../coordination/DECISIONS.md), and [status](../coordination/STATUS.md) only as the result justifies.

## Ethical and public boundaries

Use synthetic households for modeling. Keep personal medical, financial, demographic, and applicant records out of this repository. Simulation results do not select residents or establish consent. Research does not authorize purchases, legal commitments, fundraising, contacting residents or companies, or deployment.
