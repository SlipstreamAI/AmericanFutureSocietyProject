# Contributing

The most useful contribution answers a consequential question with evidence another person can inspect. A well-supported finding that an attractive idea fails is progress.

## A bounded contribution

1. Read the [project status](coordination/STATUS.md), [decisions](coordination/DECISIONS.md), relevant [department charter](departments/README.md), and [research method](research/METHOD.md).
2. Define one question, its intended decision, scope, and deliverable. Use the [research brief](templates/RESEARCH-BRIEF.md) and record a task in the [work log](coordination/WORK-LOG.md).
3. Work from current main on one task branch. Reuse the established workspace and operator workflow. Do not overwrite another contributor's work or silently replace a different draft.
4. Put specialist findings beside the department README; put shared assumptions and decisions in their canonical registers. Link across them rather than copying numbers into multiple incompatible versions.
5. Check units, dates, geography, arithmetic, sources, uncertainty, local links, and implications for other departments. A code or model change also needs appropriate executable validation.
6. Summarize the result in a commit and review request. Close the task with actual checks and a clear handoff. Follow operator instructions for pushing, integration, and cleanup.

## Writing research

- State the question and answer first; show the supporting evidence and limits.
- Prefer direct records, official datasets, audited accounts, published methods, and current jurisdiction-specific documents. Read what you cite.
- Identify the period and geography, not just the date you downloaded a source.
- Show the mechanism: who owns the asset, who pays whom, who bears costs and risk, and what remains available to households.
- Distinguish an arithmetic example, a documented analogue, a calibrated model, and a tested operating outcome.
- Keep a serious alternative and the strongest objection visible. Do not manufacture agreement among specialist agents.
- Mark material assumptions by their register ID. Revise or supersede them when better evidence arrives.

## Public repository boundaries

Publish concise project material that the operator authorizes. Do not commit raw private transcripts, personal applicant or resident information, medical or financial records, credentials, private access links, large datasets, or vendor-confidential material. Link to allowed public sources and record provenance. Obtain any permission the operator requires before external actions.

## Templates and review

Use the [templates](templates/README.md) when they save effort; adapt them to the task. A review should check fidelity to the vision, source support, accounting consistency, resource constraints, effects on people, and whether the result changes the shared status. [Licensing](LICENSE-STATUS.md) remains an operator decision.
