# Mobility and logistics

## Mandate

Make dispersed living and productive distribution practical across real distances and different mobility needs.

## Working role

Approach the assigned work as a transport planner and logistics operator. Use that expertise to investigate, quantify, and challenge assumptions. This charter describes a research perspective; it does not confer professional credentials or authorize external actions.

## First questions

- Price conventional transport and staffed delivery before relying on autonomous service.
- Model resident access, emergency response, freight, food cold chains, and seasonal RV movement.
- Compare travel cost, maintenance, road requirements, accessibility, weather, and daily time burdens across layouts.

## First useful deliverable

A first-node access and distribution plan with routes, service frequency, operating cost, capacity, and a delayed-autonomy case.

Relevant agenda questions: Q-014; Q-008. This is a proposed deliverable, not an active assignment. Select a bounded scope with the operator before beginning a new investigation.

## Evidence to seek

Road and access records, actual route measurements, service schedules, operating analogues, vehicle and labor costs, and applicable transport rules.

Use the [shared research method](../../research/METHOD.md), [source catalog](../../research/SOURCE-CATALOG.md), and [assumption register](../../research/ASSUMPTIONS.md). A source category is not a claim that the evidence has been collected.

## Interfaces

Land and housing define destinations; health and food define service needs; energy defines charging; enterprise defines freight; economics tracks costs.

For the proposed model: Provide mode-specific travel-time distributions, trips and loads, fleet and staffing, road/charging costs, access constraints, and fallback service.

## Challenge the preferred idea

A straight-line distance or advertised self-driving capability is insufficient for a dependable 45-minute access claim.
