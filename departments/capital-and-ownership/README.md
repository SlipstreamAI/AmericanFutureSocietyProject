# Capital and ownership

## Mandate

Explain who funds each stage, who owns which rights, and how losses and gains reach different participants.

## Working role

Approach the assigned work as a project finance analyst and ownership-design researcher. Use that expertise to investigate, quantify, and challenge assumptions. This charter describes a research perspective; it does not confer professional credentials or authorize external actions.

## First questions

- Compare founder equity, resident capital, cooperative or trust structures, external investors, debt, and other plausible funding sources.
- Model startup cash, working capital, debt service, dilution, distributions, and long-lived asset replacement.
- Investigate resident entry and exit, vesting, intergenerational claims, newcomers, and fair treatment when people cannot work.

## First useful deliverable

A staged capital stack and ownership alternatives memo with entity-level cash waterfalls, investor/resident rights, and unresolved legal questions.

Relevant agenda questions: Q-006; Q-005. This is a proposed deliverable, not an active assignment. Select a bounded scope with the operator before beginning a new investigation.

## Evidence to seek

Comparable financing terms and ownership documents, credible lender/investor requirements, audited examples, and jurisdiction-specific professional analysis.

Use the [shared research method](../../research/METHOD.md), [source catalog](../../research/SOURCE-CATALOG.md), and [assumption register](../../research/ASSUMPTIONS.md). A source category is not a claim that the evidence has been collected.

## Interfaces

Enterprise and housing define capital needs; governance evaluates structures; economics reconciles distributions; synthesis compares affordability with solvency.

For the proposed model: Provide capital sources/uses, timing, financing terms, ownership percentages, actual cash entitlements, restrictions, reserves, and downside allocation.

## Challenge the preferred idea

An ownership percentage does not necessarily mean an equal share of cash. A funding aspiration is not committed financing.
