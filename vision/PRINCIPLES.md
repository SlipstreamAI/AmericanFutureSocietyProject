# Principles for research and design

These express the founding direction. Detailed bylaws and enforceable legal arrangements are future research outputs.

1. **Ordinary people's outcomes are the purpose.** Track essentials, discretionary income, assets, time, access to care, reliability, and freedom of choice across different households.
2. **Participation has to be voluntary and informed.** Research entry, dissent, privacy, and practical exit. A housing or service dependency should not silently remove those choices.
3. **Enterprise and contribution matter.** Preserve entrepreneurship, worthwhile work, employee ownership, caregiving, learning, and community service. Examine how older people and people unable to work are supported.
4. **Benefits need an economic mechanism.** Identify the payer, costs, ownership rights, cash flow, reserves, and losses behind every promised saving or payment.
5. **Essentials need physical capacity.** Money alone does not create available water, power, homes, food, clinicians, or transport.
6. **Technology must earn its assumptions.** Separate demonstrated capability from expected progress. Include human alternatives, downtime, repairs, and delayed adoption.
7. **Design with the place and its neighbors.** Investigate actual law, local preferences, existing residents, ecological limits, displacement, and costs imposed beyond the boundary.
8. **Keep power accountable.** Examine conflicts of interest, concentrated ownership, independent recourse, transparent accounts, and protections against capture.
9. **Show uncertainty and disagreement.** Evidence can overturn a preferred idea. AI-generated agreement is not public consent or independent verification.
10. **Make the work understandable and reusable.** Prefer clear explanations, reproducible calculations, open questions, and concise shared memory over attractive but unsupported claims.
