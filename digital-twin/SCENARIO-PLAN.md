# Scenario plan

These are proposed experiment designs, not completed runs. Use the [scenario template](../templates/SCENARIO.md) when an operator-authorized modeling task begins.

## Compare coherent alternatives

| Scenario family | What it investigates | Required comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimal parcel pilot | Shared logistics/services and optional hosting at a small scale | Conventional living and procurement with the same households |
| Town-adjacent node | Use of existing services and infrastructure | Added capacity, fees, local effects, and alternatives to building new |
| Larger planned node | Whether scale improves economics enough to cover new infrastructure | Startup capital, demand, absorption, governance, and downside risk |
| Connected regions | Seasonal mobility, trade, and shared capabilities | Coordination, travel, duplicated infrastructure, and portable entitlements |

Population cases such as 5,000 and 50,000 belong in a sensitivity set, not as established endpoints. A much smaller pilot is also possible. Horizon controls may show 30, 50, or 100 years, but evidence quality and structural uncertainty must be disclosed for each period.

## Stress cases

No anchor customer; lower margins and demand; delayed construction and occupancy; higher interest and insurance costs; constrained water; energy outages and peak shortfalls; slower automation; major maintenance/replacement; resident turnover and changing age mix; disagreement over entry/exit; and coupled drought, utility, and finance stress.

Include a zero-dividend case and a conventional-technology case. Essential services and household budgets should expose shortfalls rather than maintain promised outputs by assumption.

## Outcomes

Household cushion and essential costs by cohort; assets and debt; entry/exit affordability; service access and reliability; work and travel time; enterprise viability; community and government solvency; resource use; replacement funding; exposure to outside customers and financing; and the distribution of benefits and costs.

Report where a design is dominated, where tradeoffs remain, and what new evidence would most reduce uncertainty. Do not collapse the answer into an unexplained happiness or feasibility score.
