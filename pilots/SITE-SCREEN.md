# Site-screening method

## Search hypotheses

Arizona, New Mexico, Nevada, and California City were named in the brainstorm. They are unscreened candidates, not findings about affordability, local permission, water, or suitability. Include a comparator outside the original set to test the premise.

## Use two passes

**Pass 1: critical evidence.** For each candidate, record confirmed, contradicted, or unknown for legal access, feasible residential use, dependable and legally available water, service/utility path, hazard exposure, land control, and a realistic financing/development path. Unknown is not a pass. A contradicted condition can trigger an exclusion or a clearly costed alternative design.

**Pass 2: tradeoffs.** Compare total serviced cost, expansion, nearby healthcare and schools, broadband, transport corridors, productive advantages, climate/resource fit, household travel time, existing community effects, and local interest where actual evidence exists. Keep criteria visible. Do not average a fatal water or occupancy problem away with cheap acreage.

## Evidence columns for a screen

Candidate and boundary; criterion; finding; source and date; geographic fit; confidence; unknowns; cost or constraint implication; owner department; next discriminating check. Use the [site dossier](../templates/SITE-DOSSIER.md).

If a score is later useful, publish definitions, weights, missing-data rules, and sensitivity; report critical constraints separately. Do not assign weights as though they were the operator's preferences.
